How this works

Method & limitations

13Forage answers one question: which publicly-observable institutional managers carry exposure to specific late-stage private companies? The catch is that the obvious data source can't answer it directly.

What a 13F does and doesn't show

Form 13F requires large institutional managers to disclose their holdings of "13(f) securities" every quarter — but that category is essentially US-listed equities, ADRs, and certain options and convertible notes. A fund's direct equity in a private company like OpenAI or Anthropic is not a 13(f) security, so it never appears in the 13F holdings table. Reading 13Fs alone will never surface private exposure.

Where the signal actually lives

Private exposure becomes publicly observable through other channels: listed investment trusts and closed-end funds that report private holdings, publicly-traded holding companies, BDCs, and fund literature filed or published for investors. 13Forage's analytical layer is a hand-curated map of those relationships, drawn from public reporting and marked by how well each claim is substantiated.

The live layers

Two things update in real time, fetched directly in your browser with no server in between. First, each fund's latest 13F-HR filing date and links come from SEC EDGAR's full-text search endpoint, keeping filing metadata current. Second, a one-month price chart and percentage change for each fund's listed proxy come from Stooq. The structured 13F holdings tables are not fetched live, because the SEC endpoints that serve them don't permit direct browser access — that analysis stays in the curated dataset.

What the charts do and don't show

A chart tracks a listed proxy, never the fund's private stakes. Some funds are themselves listed — Destiny Tech100 trades as DXYZ, the ARK ETFs as ARKK and ARKVX — so their charts track the actual vehicle, though even then a closed-end fund's price can swing to a large premium or discount versus the value of what it holds. For private partnerships with no ticker, there is simply nothing to chart, and the site says so rather than inventing a number. A proxy's price movement reflects its whole portfolio and market sentiment, not the performance of any single private position.

Not investment advice. 13Forage is an independent aggregator for research and education. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell. Private-company exposure is inferred, often from second-hand reporting, and may be stale, partial, or wrong. Always verify against primary sources before acting.
